Happy Thanksgiving! I have much to be grateful for: my wonderful friends, family, education, breath of life, a mind that is always thinking (this, I would argue, is both a blessing and a curse), and much more. It is often easy to take things for granted. But this mindset makes me think, should I be grateful for something simply because others lack that very thing? My goal is not to come across ungrateful, but instead to merely analyze how much of our lives is determined not by our own choices or feelings, but by our position relative to others.

I should be grateful for having food, clothing, shelter, a good education, etc., *because* others are less fortunate than me and lack one or more of these things. I have a good education, and thus I should pursue all of the opportunities presented to me and eventually get a good job, *because* others work so hard but have not been able to get as far as I have. I should be excited to wake up in the morning and go to class/work/responsibilities, *because* others are less fortunate than I am.

As much as I agree about the spirit of gratefulness and not taking things for granted, these “moral obligations” (for lack of a better term) give us a duty based on the social positions we have relative to another person or group. So then is this mindset intrinsic, or rather conditional on our relative position (social/monetary/otherwise)? If everyone in the world had access to food, shelter, and a good education, would these things suddenly become less worthy of our gratitude? Surely the value of something is not entirely dependent on someone else's deprivation of it.

But this idea of relativeness extends far beyond how we determine what we ought to feel. It also seems to shape our perception of who we actually are. More broadly, consider the character traits that would define you. Kind? Funny? Quiet? Well, are you a kind person, or simply kinder than the perceived “average person”? Are you funny, or just funnier than the lot? Or quieter than most? How much of what I perceive to be the traits that make up my personality are actually conditional on my position in comparison to others (conscious or otherwise)? Perhaps kindness does not necessarily require me to be kinder than someone else, but it does require something outside of myself toward which I can direct that kindness. Either way, something beyond myself seems necessary for me to make sense of who I am.

Now take this a step further and remove not only other people, but everything else. If I existed in a vacuum, with no others around, could I still be considered kind? Funny? Quiet? What would be my point of reference? Who would I direct my kindness to? If all I ever knew was my own consciousness, personality, and way of being, would I still put labels on these parts of “myself,” or would I just *be*?

The point I am trying to make is that our individuality, the traits that make us actually *us*, and so many more labels we cling so strongly to often only arise from our existence in relation to others and the world around us. I cannot be me without those around me. To a further extent, can we even distinguish a “me” in the absence of others?

Perhaps, then, what I should be most grateful for this Thanksgiving is not necessarily what I have that others lack, but the very existence of others. Those who, consciously or otherwise, have contributed to my understanding of who I am.
